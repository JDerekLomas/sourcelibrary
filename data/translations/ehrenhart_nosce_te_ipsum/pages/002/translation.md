But that / to cure all illnesses thoroughly, whatever names they may have / a single medicine is sufficient / and that to attain such a thing a very compendious instruction can be given / from which one can learn everything / as from a center / I will now briefly demonstrate in the following man-ner.

The physician's foremost subject is man / therefore a physician can study nothing better than the Nofce te ipsum, which the wise heathens as their sole purpose not without deeper mystery placed over their Temple of Apollo / by which they at the same time indicated / that this know-ledge cannot be thoroughly attained without the illumination of the divine light.

But when man through the grace of God begins to consider himself physico-medicinally / he sees firstly / that all different parts or members of his body take their origin from a single primä materiä spermaticā. For if these different parts were not in this spermate duplicato / they could not possibly arise therefrom.

These members now / sprung from a single primä materiä / in their diveris substantiis or effentus with their different functionibus or powers, take their nourishment also only from a single food / exempli gratia, from bread. So there must necessarily also be in this single food the essential nourishment of all the different members.
